# Otázka k písemnému zodpovězení E-003422/2021: French law reinforcing compliance with the principles of the French Republic

Otázka k písemnému zodpovězení E-003422/2021 (E-9-2021-003422), podáno 2. 7. 2021. Autor: Marcel Kolaja. Adresát: Evropská komise. Otázka europoslance je jeho vyjádření, ne stanovisko strany; odpověď je odpověď orgánu EU.

## Otázka

*Text v jazyce: angličtina (český překlad EP nezveřejnil).*

Subject: French law reinforcing compliance with the principles of the French Republic

Following the publication of the Commission proposal for a regulation on a Digital Services Act (DSA), the French Government decided in January 2021 to introduce, in an anticipative manner, certain provisions of the DSA regulation in the bill on the respect of the principles of the French Republic. In its draft law, the French Government introduces changes that question, among other things, the principle of country of origin, as a justification for its anticipative implementation of the DSA.

The DSA Regulation is currently still being examined by Parliament and the Council.

As the French Government has notified the Commission of these provisions, will the Commission decide to impose a standstill period of 18 months for the French bill to avoid legislative overlaps and fragmentation?

## Odpověď

Odpověď (Evropská komise) ze dne 19. 8. 2021: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/E-9-2021-003422-ASW_CS.html

*Text v jazyce: angličtina.*

Answer given by Mr Breton

on behalf of the European Commission

(19.8.2021)

Following the procedure set out in Directive (EU) 2015/1535, France notified the Commission of its draft law on “reinforcing compliance with the principles of the Republic” which would impose greater responsibilities on certain online service providers to prevent the spread of content that incites violence and hate online.

The Commission issued comments on 14 June 2021 on some aspects of the draft law, with a view to ensuring that it does not hinder the smooth functioning of the EU Single Market. The notified draft also raised concerns in view of the Commission’s proposal on the Digital Services Act[1] and the adopted Regulation on terrorist content online[2]. It is now for France to take these comments into account.

More generally, the Commission shares the objective of combating illegal content online through proportionate means, which safeguard fundamental rights, including freedom of speech. When legislating on these issues, Member States have an obligation to comply with existing EU rules such as the e-Commerce Directive, which includes provisions on the country of origin principle.

The Commission further recalls that once the Digital Services Act is adopted, Member States are obliged by Union law not to - and are in fact prevented from - legislating in the area and in relation to the objectives pursued by the harmonising rules of EU legislation.

The Commission is committed to working closely with Member States and the co-legislators throughout the negotiation of the Digital Services Act, with a view to agreeing to impactful EU-wide rules.

[1] Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on a Single Market For Digital Services (Digital Services Act) and amending Directive 2000/31/EC, COM/2020/825

[2] Regulation (EU) 2021/784 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2021 on addressing the dissemination of terrorist content online, PE/19/2021/INIT, OJ L 172, 17.5.2021
