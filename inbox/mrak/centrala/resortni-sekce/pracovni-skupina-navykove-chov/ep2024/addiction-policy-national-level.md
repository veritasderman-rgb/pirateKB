# Addiction Policy National Level

> Převedeno z mraku (`Centrala/Resortní sekce/Pracovní skupina Návykové chování/EP2024/Addiction_Policy_National_Level.odt`). Neověřený návrh, čeká na kurátora.

Introduction

Addiction to addictive substances or gambling negatively affects the life not only of a person, but also of his or her family, those around him or her, and has negative effects on the whole of society. It is therefore desirable to prevent such phenomena to the maximum extent possible, which Pirates will advocate. To succeed, a realistic approach must be adopted, based on verified data, good practice, the latest expert knowledge, and effective involvement of a wide range of professionals from all different fields - social, health or educational. It is essential to balance regulation of the availability of addictive substances with a well-targeted offer of prevention and treatment. Scientific studies show that a certain percentage of the population is prone to problem addictive behaviour. Thus, even extremely strict restrictions cannot produce a 100% result, on the contrary (the most famous example is the failed prohibition of alcohol in the US in the 1920s). The idea of a "drug-free society" promised by populists is thus unrealistic. But this does not mean that it is not possible to significantly reduce the incidence of risky addictive behaviour in society and limit its potential harm. But an effective, well-tested approach must be adopted. What should an effective approach look like?​ The policy of preventing the emergence of risky addictive behaviour and reducing the associated harm (hereinafter "policy") must be based on a scientifically proven, balanced and realistic concept of risk prevention and harm reduction (i.e. the principle of harm reduction​¹ - HR). The primary aim is to reduce the risk of developing risky addictive behaviour and, if it occurs, to mediate realistic interventions aimed at comprehensive assistance to the person and harm minimisation. The trend towards abstinence is intended to be part of the offer, not the sole or main objective. In some cases, it may not be possible to achieve it, and excessive pressure in this direction only discourages the addict from trying to work with the problem and bring it more effectively under control. The main objectives of the policy are to:

to minimise negative impacts on public health and the health of individuals;

to minimise negative impacts on the social situation of individuals, families and local communities;

to minimise negative impacts on the public budget;

to protect public order;

to maintain the independence of state institutions in the context of the influence of representatives of the "addiction" industry.

The so-called balanced approach is considered the most effective policy. It assumes a balance between regulation and preventive-therapeutic offers. A free market without regulations and excessive repression or over-regulation inadvertently worsens the situation in the form of negative impacts on health, public budgets​² or safety risks. Funding, legislation and specific interventions should be directed more towards citizens, who are more at risk of developing risky addictive behaviour, to early detection of the problem and to early intervention. General prevention often misses the point of experience, as mentioned above. Integrated policy is an effective modern approach in addictive behaviour policy. This approach sees the interconnectedness and inseparability of the use of various types of addictive substances, whether legal or illegal, with non-substance addictions such as pathological gambling, and their interaction. It also reflects other related areas such as drug abuse, addictive behaviour in the environment of new technologies, social networks, etc. The phenomenon of addictive behaviour extends into the social, economic, health and safety areas; interconnectedness of these areas must be an essential part of the policy. It is essential that the government has a long-term strategy covering all relevant sectors and sectors. The policy of preventing the emergence of risky addictive behaviour and reducing the damage associated with it must be managed in a coordinated manner and the government (if it is represented by the Pirate Party) will pay attention to it at the highest possible level.⁴ For effective coordination, it is crucial to support and develop the highest possible involvement of self-government, without which it is impossible to create community cooperation. Self-government is an essential coordinating point in creating a network of assistance and identifying needs for community development.

It must also be reflected that the area of addictive behaviour does not respect national borders. It is therefore essential to be an active participant on the international scene​⁵​ and to promote all the above principles. The Pirate Party will strive for the rational liberalisation of so-called drug policies in the sense of the revision of the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs of 1961. At EU level, the Pirate Party will promote the above-mentioned principles of harm reduction both in EU strategies, directives and action plans. At the same time, there is a need to modernise approaches with regard to policy integration also at the practical level of individual Directorates-General of the European Commission (the so-called DG – Directorate General), to define budgets from the EU level of grants and tenders and to extend the mandate of the European Monitoring Centre for Drugs and Drug Addiction (EMCDDA ) for monitoring the whole issue of addictive behaviour.

In conclusion, the Pirate Party promotes the so-called evidence-based approach, i.e. an approach based on scientific evidence. Our policy of preventing the emergence of risky addictive behaviour and reducing the damage associated with it will be based on monitoring​⁶, facts and examples of good practice on the one hand, and on the competence of executing actors across individual sectors​ on the other hand. It will be based on a clear analysis of the monitored data and look for the optimal solution of a balanced approach between regulatory measures and the offer of prevention and treatment. On the basis of the evidence, we will then create a draft state budget for the given area in cooperation with municipalities and health and social insurance systems.

1. ​Legislation

a) Problems of the current framework

The illegality of certain psychotropic substances inevitably causes a reduced effectiveness of efforts to rationally prevent and reduce risks in the area of addictive behaviours, deprives the state of considerable resources that could be spent on prevention and reduction of risks and, last but not least, allows the black market to prosper.

Assisting services in the area of addictive behaviours, otherwise called addiction services, are always of a health-social character. However, the current legislation strictly divides them into health and social services - there is no legislative anchor in the area of social-health boundaries.

The current network of assisting services is focused primarily on users of illegal drugs and care in the area of alcohol, tobacco and addictive behaviours in general is not sufficiently provided. The structure of the network of services is also unsatisfactory, there is a lack of outpatient and especially low-threshold care in the area of alcohol. There is a lack of a comprehensive definition of the basic network of addictive services and its anchoring in the legislation.

Despite the same legislation, the individual regions take different approaches to the creation of regional networks of addictive services and their financing. Moreover, the whole process is extremely administratively demanding and does not sufficiently reflect the real needs of clients and also does not allow new types of services to emerge - there is a lack of a legislative solution for the uniform and transparent multi-annual financing of addictive services. It is therefore necessary to find a clear financial mechanism and to stop funding this type of care primarily by means of subsidies. At the same time, it is necessary to redirect current resources from health insurance into specialised types of health care.

The current tax system, including the so-called tax package, does not sufficiently reflect - for example by graduating the tax rate - the social dangers of individual commodities (alcohol, tobacco, gambling).

The legislative setting of the legal regulation of advertising underestimates the dangers posed by alcoholic beverages, especially for the younger generation, and thus contributes to the general acceptance of alcohol as a normal part of everyday life. This needs to be changed.

b) What changes the Pirates propose

Steps towards the regulated legalisation of hitherto illegal addictive substances​⁸ are necessary. The current state of affairs allows the continuous flourishing of the black market and makes it impossible to provide effective assistance to those who need it most.

We want to recodify tax laws so that they reflect the real social dangers of individual commodities and individual types of gambling.

It is necessary to open a discussion on the unification of the tax on alcohol, because at present, for example, still wine has zero tax, but cask beer has 10% VAT. It is also a question of how high the rate should be compared to pharmaceuticals, which currently also have 10%. The Pirate Party will initiate a debate and propose possible solutions - a variant may be a tax on a gram of alcohol in individual beverages, which would lead to unification of excise taxes on alcohol.

Part of the adjustment of the tax system should be the introduction of the possibility of a purposeful determination of tax revenues arising from the taxation of addictive substances, gambling, etc. These revenues should be primarily intended for financing activities in the area of prevention of risky addictive behavior and reduction of the damage associated with it.

In the area of gambling, it is necessary to carry out a revision of the gambling law and to make its regular evaluation in terms of new trends (for example, the increase of online gambling) and current impacts. Impact monitoring contains the annual annual report of the National Monitoring Centre, which is approved by the government of the Czech Republic.

In order to protect health, safety and public order, it is necessary to support more so-called harm reduction programs. Thus, for example, to strengthen and expand the possibilities of using substitution preparations (this is a medicine prescribed by a doctor, which has similar effects in the body as the original substance used, but without a number of health risks, just as substitution therapy reduces the risk of criminality). ​. ​It is also necessary to promote the existence of so-called application rooms, which contribute to reducing the undesirable consequences of substance use and limit their application in public places​.

We will prepare a legislative definition of a health social service, ideally in a separate act on a financial mechanism (for example, the Agency for the Prevention of Addictions, following the model of the British National treatmemt agency), or by supplementing Act 65/2017 Coll. on the Protection of Health against the Harmful Effects of Addictive Substances. With this, we will modify the related legislative definitions of the typology of addiction services and establish optimal networks of services, including financing.

In the field of alcohol advertising, the Pirate Party will advocate a reasonable change of the Advertising Act, which would better reflect the risks of alcohol in relation to both adults and minors. In the field of tobacco, on the contrary, within the HR approach, we rather propose the release of advertising for nicotine/tobacco use alternatives.

c) Human rights

Pirate policy in relation to risky addictive behaviour and its prevention takes into account both the health of individuals and their fundamental rights and freedoms. In the context of compulsory public health and social insurance, it is also necessary to take into account the impacts on public budgets. In this sense, the policy must delicately balance the necessary preventive regulation and individual freedom - the respect of fundamental human rights.

The Pirate Party will sensitively seek ways to regulate the availability of alcohol, tobacco, gambling and other addictive substances, so that especially people under the age of 18 are protected.

Attention will also be paid to specific target groups: gender issues (women - drug users), ageing users of addictive substances, ethnic minorities, prisoners.

​ 2. ​Specific areas

a) Security issues

The repressive and regulatory aspects should be the ultimate solution not only in the legal but also in the factual sense. The blanket criminalization of an individual exhibiting signs of risky addictive behaviour is counterproductive. The repressive components should effectively co-operate in the area of prevention of risky behaviour leading to criminal delinquency.

The general approach in the area of security should have a balanced multidisciplinary and primarily preventive character with an emphasis on the awareness of risk groups and the public. Multidisciplinarity should be enforced outside the Police of the Czech Republic (it is one of the subjects of action) and inside (strengthening the co-ordination of the various police services: in particular the criminal police and investigation services, the riot police services and the foreign police services).

Repressive and regulatory measures should be subject to permanent monitoring and evaluation on the basis of predetermined indicators of success. Indicators and their evaluation should be related to a clearly defined decrease in unwanted phenomena in society, similar to other regulators. Indicators in the area of criminal law cannot be based only on the number of completed criminal proceedings, the number of seizures of illegally manufactured or smuggled addictive substances, etc.

Target groups for action in the area of repression/regulation should be differentiated. Focus on better prevention and information especially in relation to low-age risk groups and predisposed individuals. Effective repression against illegal sale of addictive substances is needed, balanced by state regulation of commercial market with them.

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends several basic regulatory measures, which Pirates supports, based on studies of successful policies in the area of legal addictive substances:

Restriction of availability - prohibition of sales and administration up to a certain age, restriction of time and place of sale, restriction of advertising.

Price formation - taxation by means of indirect taxes, such as excise taxes​.¹⁰

Restriction of situations and places of consumption - for example, prohibition of smoking in designated places, prohibition of driving after consumption of alcohol and other addictive substances limiting attention, etc.

From the point of view of security risks, it is necessary to continue the involvement of the Security Information Service (BIS) in the analytical work leading to the emergence of other specific measures, which will have the aim of limiting the negative influence of lobby groups representing multinational corporations on the creation or changes of legislation on addictive behaviour. Not only in the area of gambling, but also in the area of tobacco​¹​ and possibly alcohol.

When enforcing the current laws, it is necessary to count on the involvement of other control mechanisms and institutions. In addition to the Police of the Czech Republic, it is also the customs administration, the CTIA, trade and financial offices, sanitation, fire brigade, local governments and others. For real effectiveness of regulation, it is necessary that the measures have a non-punitive character and their enforcement is solved in cooperation of the mentioned institutions.

b) Tax issues

The tax system in the area of the policy of preventing the emergence of risky addictive behavior and reducing the damage associated with it does not mean only excise tax and value added tax. It has to be perceived figuratively as a complex system of regulatory instruments that brings resources for financing preventive measures and for expenses related to possible negative externalities. When setting up tax instruments, it is necessary to analyze their impact comprehensively, i.e. taking into account other factors.

One of the objectives of the taxation of consumption is to limit consumption as such due to its negative externalities. Excise taxes on addictive substances cannot be evaluated from the point of view of collection into the state budget, but as the elimination of risk phenomena in different population groups, respectively reducing the prevalence of use of individual types of substances.

In accordance with the HR principle, it is necessary to set up the tax policy system so as to motivate users to satisfy their needs by activities with the lowest possible risk of pathological addiction.

From the point of view of the economic approach, it is socially responsible and desirable to implement the maximum volume of transactions related to addictive substances in the official economy, where both market parties are subject to a higher level of control, but also protection from the standard risks of the black market. It is necessary to break the taboo of debate about strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats of legalization, both from the point of view of the fiscal and from the point of view of the policy of preventing the emergence of risky addictive behaviour and reducing the damage associated with it.

It is necessary to implement tax (and other) tools that will bring social benefits into the state's tax policy settings. We are talking here, for example, about financing activities in the field of drug policy in the form of tax-deductible costs, the creation of a system of tax assimilation, the possibility to implement corporate social responsibility programs in state-defined groups of the most needy projects with the possibility to obtain a benefit in contact with the state administration.

The optimal solution in the field of taxation of alcoholic beverages appears to be the introduction of a tax on the unit amount of alcohol contained in a beverage – see above in the paragraph of the legislation.

In the field of taxation of gambling, the appropriate solution is to differentiate the tax according to the social dangers of the various types of gambling – the lowest tax on lotteries, the highest tax on technical games.

On the expenditure side, it must be ensured that a clearly defined part of the proceeds of excise taxes on both tobacco and alcoholic beverages (and in the future also on other psychotropic substances) and part of taxes on gambling is devoted to the prevention and treatment of addictions.

Another way to increase tax revenues usable for prevention and reduction of risks is to allow so-called tax assimilations. This is a concept where the taxpayer has a direct possibility to influence the targeting of the proceeds of his own income tax.

c) Anti-corruption issues

The issue of corruption is closely related to the topic of addictive substances and gambling. Unfortunately, according to Transparency International and the Council of Europe's anti-corruption group GRECO​ the Czech Republic lags behind the EU average in the fight against corruption. The focus of anti-corruption activity lies in the area of non-state organisations, which find it difficult to find partners in the state administration.

Anti-corruption measures are fragmented, the state administration and self-government are prone to tolerate corrupt behaviour; the fight against corruption is unpopular and is marginalised by the actors involved. Corruption is underestimated and becomes the "norm", as a result of which it degrades the effectiveness of the policy.

Enforcing transparency and monitoring within e-Government must become a powerful tool in the anti-corruption fight.

Support is needed for special police forces operating centrally at the national level. Today, they specialise only in the "most serious" cases of legalisation of criminal proceeds, while addressing these attacks on the state's fiscal policy must be supported by specialists at regional and local level.

The anti-corruption initiative is to be supported and protected to the maximum by making use of effective measures provided for by law.

For gambling: ​ The greatest corruption potential lies in the possibility for municipalities to authorise gambling on their territory; the aim should be to introduce legislation that will regulate the conditions for the operation of stone establishments across the board so that municipalities can decide on the authorisation on the basis of clear and transparent rules provided for by law.

For alcohol:​ In the case where municipalities themselves can by decrees determine where alcohol can be sold and where it can't, there is again more scope for corruption. Paradoxically, a greater level of corruption can be expected even after the onset of greater repression regarding pouring to minors. In this case, however, it is more advantageous for society to take on a greater level of corruption risks, as the Czech Republic has a large and acute problem in the area of alcoholism that needs to be urgently addressed.

For tobacco:​ A certain amount of tobacco in our country comes from the black market. From the point of view of preventing corruption risks, the key is:

To obtain data and analyse the real impact of the black market; on the basis of this information to evaluate the actions in the area of combating organised crime, where it is likely that billions of dollars of evasion of excise duties on tobacco are involved.

It is necessary to identify the tobacco industry as one of the sectors with the greatest corruption potential​²​ in the area of influencing legislation. The Pirate Party will seek resources for analysing the interconnectedness of organised crime, the tobacco industry and politics.

Legislatively, the Pirate Party within HR will support the transition of nicotine users from tobacco products to e-cigarettes and similar alternatives.

In the area of currently illegal psychotropic substances:​ It is necessary to protect consumers by allowing the emergence of a market for psychotropic substances, which will be rationally regulated, controlled and properly taxed. In the area of therapeutic use of psychotropic substances, it is necessary to prevent possible corruption efforts by the pharmaceutical lobby. To minimise the impact, it is necessary to move strictly to the HR​​¹³​ approach and to the controlled medical administration of certain drugs (for example heroin or methamphetamine) to addicts following the example of other countries (Great Britain, Switzerland)​¹ ⁴ so that both the dealer and the illegal producer are removed from the process. Regulated legalisation of other illegal substances should also be discussed. The Pirate Party will initiate government studies and a debate across professional communities (health social, safety, economic or with the judiciary) on their possible strictly controlled availability. Prohibition in this sense does not fulfil its purpose and creates a completely corrupt environment.

For the area of legislation:​ All proposals regarding addiction must pass through the government's advisory body - the Government Council for Coordination of Drug Policy - and its other subordinate bodies, even at the stage of making a substantive intention. Only in this way can they be under the greatest possible professional control; this can reduce the occurrence of corruptly influenced laws and their parts.

d) Other related issues

Another shortcoming today is the lack of provision of assisting services in the natural environment of the client. An underdeveloped network of services for children and adolescents as well as the unsatisfactory state of further education for addictive health professions. The Pirate Party will initiate at different levels the solution of these challenges and seek the means to ensure them.

3. ​Allocation of funds

a) Problems of the current framework

There is no uniform concept of creating a network of assisting services including the funding system.

The existing funding through the grant titles of various donors is unpredictable, complicated, unclear and administratively disproportionately demanding.

The grant titles are mostly one-year, even for long-term verified functioning necessary services. This increases the uncertainty of the services in question whether they will receive the necessary funds for the given year.

The requirements for the settlement of grant funds are irrational and often do not allow the financing of current operating expenses.

Each donor has specific requirements for the reporting system.

The current subsidy system makes it almost impossible for services to develop and does not offer space to respond to the current situation in the regions.

The ratio between preventive therapeutic activities and the punitive component is not set rationally.

​ b) What the Pirates propose

Changing the funding system

The government is to ensure a stable system of funding for a network of preventive, relief and treatment services; funding for all areas is to be based on clear indicators aimed at meeting predefined objectives.

A stable network of assisting services must be created, respecting the needs defined in cooperation with the county and municipalities; it is primarily necessary to change the financing of the network, now financed to a significant extent on the basis of subsidy programmes, and to switch to systemic contractual obligations.

It is estimated that it is necessary to gradually increase the funds from the state budget for prevention and treatment to the level of approximately 1% from excise taxes on alcohol and tobacco and taxes on gambling (this amounts to approximately CZK 750 million). CZK a year).

The government will initiate the creation of a national treatment and prevention agency, which will centralize the funds of the ministries, establish contractual relations with the municipalities and, on the basis of clearly defined national and local priorities, ensure stable, multiannual and predictable financing of the basic network of care. The aim of such funding must be to establish clear indicators of the success of individual interventions.

It is also necessary to change the financing from health insurance so that these funds are used efficiently.

​ 4. ​Coordination

a) Declaration of the principle of subsidiarity

The policy of preventing the emergence of risky addictive behaviour and reducing the associated damage, according to Pirates, should significantly transfer some possibilities and competences to the self-government level, including the principle of co-decision on the financing of local policies and the creation of legislation by means of local decrees.

b) State

The Government Council: ​ The Government establishes the Council of the Government for the Coordination of Drug Policy (RVKPP), whose members are ministers of relevant ministries, professional societies, NGOs, representatives of self-government and possibly appointed expert/advisor to the Prime Minister of the Czech Republic. The Council is to discuss all major issues of both legislative and financial nature, preparation of Action Plans, as well as current measures. Furthermore, the Council discusses the essential information arising from the annual data collection and submits reports to the Government on the state of affairs regarding drugs, other addictive substances and gambling”​⁷. From these reports and any subsequent analyses, concrete proposals for measures are to be made, or they are to be the basis for possible legislative changes. In this sense, the Pirate Party will promote the extension of the mandate of the National Monitoring Centre (NMS) to include data collection in the area of tobacco and alcohol, following the model of the annual reports on the impact of gambling and illegal drugs.

National Drug Coordinator:​ Given the enormous pressure of the most lobbying industries in the Czech Republic (tobacco, gambling and alcohol industries), it is necessary that the national coordinator be appointed by the government, be directly subordinate to the prime minister of the Czech Republic and be in the position of deputy in the regime of the state service or alternatively the government representative and the director of the department.

Creation of a network of assisting services and prevention:​ Assistance services are built on monitoring data, their analyses and knowledge of good practice both nationally and from the world. Services must have stable funding, clear indicators must be set and efficiency must be assessed accordingly. Creation of assistance services is not built on the basis of current media demand, but on the basis of actual needs identified through monitoring.

Care is defined by the network:​ Helping so-called addiction services are supposed to maintain the principle of multidisciplinarity in accordance with harm reduction. Helping interventions should focus significantly on contact with the hidden population​¹⁸​, early detection and early intervention. Helping services should not be primarily aimed at achieving abstinence, but should have a broader context of socio-health assistance. Individual operators of assisting services will be encouraged to differentiate the types of interventions. Individual types of care (primarily residential) will seek a clearer standard of co-financing of the users of the care in question; based on the evaluation, the network of care will also be completed (e.g. home detoxification, a major expansion of opioid substitution, including a substitution alternative for methamphetamine, the creation of outpatient assistance for at-risk alcohol users in the framework of early detection and early intervention, the completion of a basic network of assistance for pathological gamblers, the completion of a network of assistance in the field of tabakism, etc). It is necessary to create an evaluation and a mechanism for the eventual termination of unnecessary services so that people in need of assistance are not left without the necessary contact with experts. Furthermore, it is necessary to create proposals for the modernisation of the now defunct models of certain types of care on the borderline of health assistance and protection of public order

see the ineffective model of the seizure​¹⁹. The prevention strategy must take into account good practice aimed at advancing the age of the first experience with addictive substances and other recommendations of the WHO in the sense of HR. The government will allocate resources for informing the public about the existence of the assistance network.

c) Regions and municipalities

The National Coordinator, the RVKPP and its Secretariat will focus part of their capacity on creating stronger support for the implementation of local policies. Local policies cannot only copy the national strategy, but based on their own data, the identification of needs from the locality must set local priorities​². Local policies should look for possibilities for their own regulatory measures in the form of municipal decrees (mainly in the focus on limiting the availability of legal drugs to under-18s, in the regulation of gambling, in the regulation of phenomena associated with the use of illegal substances, such as the sex industry, etc.) and their own measures in the area of assisting and preventive services in cooperation with the state​²¹ – field work, substitution centres, application rooms, etc. In the future, municipalities should also have the possibility to regulate to some extent the availability and places of consumption of some currently illegal drugs, such as cannabis, in view of possible changes in the area of international control of so-called narcotics.​²

d) The relationship of drug policy with other state policies

Activities in the area of drug policy have a direct connection with the activities of the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MPSV) in the area of social inclusion and defence against social exclusion; there must be very close cooperation in these areas, because it is addictive behaviour that is the most frequent cause of social exclusion and vice versa.

Close cooperation of the Ministry of Health (MZ), which is preparing the reform of psychiatric care, is also very desirable, because patients with addictive disorders and other dual diagnoses​²³ form a substantial part of patients in psychiatric services.

Cooperation with the Ministry of Education, Youth and Sports (MŠMT) on the implementation of the National Strategy for the Prevention of Risky Behaviour of Children and Youth is necessary, among other things.

Cooperation at international level is also necessary, not only in advocacy for decriminalization policy or coordination in the framework of monitoring transnational criminal groups, but also in the area of development aid.

​ 5. ​International framework

In the international area, active participation is foreseen in all international forums that are part of the various international structures of which the Czech Republic is a member. The Czech Republic's international policy on addictive behavior must be active​²⁴​.

At the international level, it is crucial to perceive that the drug problem does not replicate the borders of individual states; in this sense, the Czech Republic must respond to several challenges within the international situation. For example, the extreme situation in the field of HIV/AIDS and Hepatitis C and other communicable diseases within the former Soviet Union, the import of new psychoactive substances from China and India, etc. The Czech Republic is to actively act and transfer know-how in this area.

Currently, the Pirate Party wants to bring the agenda into the next Czech EU Presidency in the second half of 2022. In this sense, the Pirate Party wants to initiate a working group at the RVKPP composed of experts (not only civil servants) who are able to bring the topics into the Presidency. The Pirate Party will have integration and change of drug policies as one of the proposed possible priorities of the Czech Presidency.

a) Global trends

The Czech Republic is to be prepared for a change in the concept of international control. The national policy is to reflect in a ten-year horizon the coming changes in the world towards mitigating and ending the full prohibition of certain substances, such as cannabis, coca leaves, cocaine, opium tinctures and other opioid preparations, classic psychedelics, etc. In this sense, legislative and political changes need to be prepared in order to preserve the HR principle. Experience from successful tobacco or alcohol regulation policies is to be transferred into the policies of regulation of some currently illegal substances; at the same time, the policy will avoid the pendulum effect (i.e. an uncontrolled open market) and will look for the so-called golden centre in the relevant measures.

b) EU, UN

Within the UN framework, the Czech Republic is a signatory to international conventions, in particular the United Nations Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs of 1961, as amended by the Protocol of 1972. The Czech Republic is a member of the Supreme Commission, the CND (Commission for Narcotic Drugs) until 2021; in this sense, the Czech Republic will actively act in areas where it is successful and also in areas that potentially threaten the territory of the Czech Republic (in particular, prevention of communicable diseases among intravenous drug users, better support for the involvement of the non-governmental sector in drug policies, more effective policy of decriminalization of drug users, integration of policies and definition of indicators in the field of law enforcement). Within the field of illegal drugs, the Czech Republic will be active at the UN in the structures of the WHO and UNAIDS, as well as in promoting a consensual position of all EU member states on the conflict of death sentences for drug offences with international conventions. In this, together with all EU members, we also promote the principle of proportionality of sentences; the Czech Republic must clearly speak out against extrajudicial executions for drug offences, for example.

The Pirate Party will make efforts at the national and European level to carry out an analysis of the scientific relevance of the Single Convention. From this analysis it is then necessary to deduce steps: for example, revision of the Single Convention, negotiation of reservations or withdrawal of the Czech Republic or EU member countries directly from this convention.

Within the UN framework, the Czech Republic is also a signatory of the World Health Organization's Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC) from 2003; here, the Czech Republic will defend the policy of a modern HR approach and at the same time it will be involved in monitoring and implementing good practice from other countries in the sense of reducing the effects of tabakism.

Within the EU framework, the Czech Republic is active within the HDG, COSI, E.M.C.D.D.A., structure within DG Sante and others​²⁵. The Czech Republic is involved in the preparation of EU strategies and action plans; within its next Presidency, the Czech Republic will promote greater interconnectedness and integration of policies across the spectrum of addictive behaviour. In this sense, the Czech Republic will prepare a proposal for its Presidency to share monitoring (within the framework of the extension of the mandate of the EMCDDA) and to share working groups on addiction.

It is necessary to find tools for humane solutions to the situation of foreigners who become dependent on an organised group from a given community and slave labour.

c) Development aid

In the framework of development aid, the Czech Republic will not stand by and, in connection with other donors and natural partners in particular in the EU, it will offer concrete assistance in the area of preventing the emergence of risky addictive behaviour and reducing the damage associated with it in the political and strategic field.

In this sense, the Czech Republic will also focus on the so-called priority countries, where it will cooperate on the development of a network of aid mainly in the area of intravenous drug users, but also on some police activities. The Pirate Party will propose to re-include Afghanistan among the priority countries as the country most affected by the so-called drug war.

d) Neighbouring states issues (present and hypothetical problems in the future)

The issue of production and distribution of currently unregulated substances (and in the future also regulated production and distribution of these substances) will be the subject of further analysis and the creation of coordinated actions. Cooperation with the police of neighbouring states and the customs administration will continue, a cooperation mechanism in the area of demand reduction will also be created; indicators need to be set to assess the success of activities in relation to demand reduction.

Methamphetamine: The Czech Republic will be active in the creation of a pan-European network in the area of tackling the growing problem of methamphetamine/methamphetamine, thus building on the measures of the EU Drugs Action Plan 2017-2021​²⁶​. This network should strengthen mutual cooperation between EU Member States and thus prevent an increase in the prevalence of risky use, production and distribution of methamphetamine in the EU and prevent the emergence of an epidemic at EU level. In this context, it is also important to continue cooperation between police forces not only of border countries in the area of precursors to methamphetamine production.
