# Důvodová zpráva EN

Explanatory memorandum
I. General
The bill amending Act no. 167/1998 Sb., Act on Addictive Substances and on
Amendments to Certain Acts, as amended (hereinafter "the Bill").
1. Reason for submission
The preparation of the Bill was preceded by a detailed survey of the current use
of cannabis in the Czech Republic and the development trends. Legislation that is
currently being prepared or discussed and that refects the modern tendency to
legalize cannabis, which is a step taken by an increasing number of countries
around the world, was taken into account. A new complex legislation regarding
the legalization of cannabis was adopted as Act no. C-45 in Canada on 7 June
2018. This pivotal act became a signifcant model for preparing the intention to
legalize a small quantity of cannabis for one's own needs in the Czech Republic.
Setting the rules for lawful handling of a small quantity of cannabis for one's own
needs, in particular regarding growing, processing and possession of cannabis,
would also change the current state of the Administrative Infractions Act,
according to which the possession of a small quantity for one's own needs is an
administrative infraction, namely under section 39 (2)(b) Act no. 167/1998 Sb.,
Act on Addictive Substances and on Amendments to Certain Acts, as amended.
This legislative process is prompted by the efort to set clear and understandable
limits for the use of cannabis for one's own needs in small quantities and thus
distinguish such activities of individual growers of cannabis and users for their
own needs that do not pose a danger to society from more serious prohibited or
even criminal activities in the form of illegal production and illegal trade in
addictive substances (e.g. unlawful production of metamfetamine/pervitin or
operating large-scale cannabis growing facilities and smuggling cannabis out of
the Czech Republic).
The proposed Act no. 167/1998 Sb., on Addictive Substances and on
Amendments to Certain Acts, as amended ("Act on Addictive Substances") is to
refect the existing prevalence of drug use in society that may not be overlooked.
In this sense, it is necessary to rectify the situation in which a great number of
Czech citizens who are otherwise without a criminal record get into confict with
the law even though their actions do not cause any, or only insignifcant, damage
or risks for society. At the same time, the state expends disproportionate funds
on fghting this phenomenon, despite the fact that this psychoactive plant has
frmly entrenched itself in the market in an obviously irrefutable way worldwide,
including the Czech Republic.
According to the latest European Drug Report 2018 prepared by the European
Monitoring  Centre for Drugs and Drug Addiction (EMCDDA), 24 million
inhabitants of the European Union between 15 and 64 used cannabis in the past
year, which represents 7.2 % of the adult population. In the EU, 26 % of the
population of the EU (i.e. 87.6 million citizens) are life-long cannabis users. Thus
cannabis is the most widely used (prohibited) addictive substance in the
European Union. In the Czech Republic, the estimate of life-long prevalence of
cannabis users is about 26.6 % of the population (2016). A comparable
prevalence rate  applies to Germany with 27.2 % of the population (2015),
Ireland 27.9 % (2015), the Netherlands 25.2 % (2016) and Austria 23.6 % (2015).
A slightly higher prevalence is identifed in Spain with 31.5 % of the population
(2015), Italy 33 % (2017), the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern
Ireland 29.6 % (2015); in France, the prevalence is noticeable at 41.4 % (2016).
In connection with the facts above, in European countries in general there is a
noticeable trend towards a changing approach to the legal regulation of the

punishability and in many cases the sentence of imprisonment is reduced or
eliminated completely for growing and possessing cannabis for one's own needs
because it has an inadequate and too broad an impact on the public, because it
criminalizes a large part of general population who do not perceive occasional
use of their own cannabis as antisocial.
Therefore, in many European countries bills are being submitted to the law-
making bodies with the aim to legalize cannabis, both through a complete
commercialization of cannabis and through the legalization of growing cannabis
and its possession for individual needs. However, as stated above, citizens obtain
this substance on the black market or they grow it themselves at home, at the
moment illegally. The latest study of the National Monitoring Centre for Drugs
and Drugs Addiction has shown that about 800 thousand people a year obtain
cannabis on the black market at least once a year, convinced that it is for
therapeutic use, due to the poor availability of legal therapeutic cannabis. Those
people, as opposed to problematic users of alcohol and other substances (e.g.
pervitin, heroin), most often are not engaged in the related criminal activities,
such as petty crime (petty theft) or frauds, violent crimes (about 80 % of violent
crimes committed under the infuence of psychotropic substances are caused by
alcohol), disorderly conduct etc. Those people do not signifcantly burden the
health and social insurance system in the Czech Republic either. The connection
to other criminal activities and the grave social and health impacts have always
been the principal argument for the complete prohibition on the international
level of this substance and of other substances. Nevertheless, all data available
show that in the case of cannabis those reasons are completely feeting.
In contrast, the risk factor of the black market is the overlap of the illegal
cannabis market with black markets trading in other, more risky psychotropic
substances, such as pervitin. That was the reason why the Dutch system of
'cofee shops' was established. In the end, through its several decades of
existence, the experiment showed that the use of cannabis successfully split
from other illegal drugs, and fnally led to such a substantial social supervision
that the prevalence of the use of this substance among the general population is
far lower than in the Czech Republic.
The ever-growing market in new synthetic psychoactive substances, including
synthetic cannabinoids (synthetic substance with little diference between the
efective and toxic, even lethal, dose, that interact with the receptors based on
the same principle as cannabis or hashish), remains a great risk. Those
substances pose much greater risks than normal cannabis and there have been
cases of death caused by those substances across the EU. The best known
among those substances is Spice, but in Asian countries, such as China and
India, every year new alternatives to those substances are synthetized and
subsequently smuggled to the EU. The amendment to the Act presented is to
mitigate such grave risks in the development of the black market with much
more hazardous substances.
Another fundamental argument is that in the Czech Republic, the estimated
number of intensive daily smokers of cannabis is about 100 thousand, and they
often buy varieties of cannabis with high THC content and very low CBD content
on the black market. Those increase the risk of anxieties, toxic psychoses etc.
that may end in hospitalizations in psychiatric hospitals. This group and the
whole society would beneft from the possibility of a substitute, broader solution.
Last but not least, it is necessary to consider whether the state is a good
manager in this situation, and not to overlook clear economic arguments. Illegal
drugs (according to the latest study available) cost about CZK 6.7 billion in 2007,
out of which the law enforcement costs were CZK 5.6 billion (ca 80%), mostly in
direct costs (the activities of law enforcement authorities, including the prison
system). Cannabis and marijuana take up at least 20 % of the capacity of law

enforcement authorities (this is a very conservative estimate). In reality, the
social costs in 2018 compared to 2007 are estimated as double (in view of the
increase of nominal prices and in view of the increase of the direct law-
enforcement costs - e.g. 10 years ago the costs of the National Drug Enforcement
Agency were about CZK 100 million per year  while now they are about CZK 200
million per year). If we add up the fgures, the present social costs of illegal drugs
are about CZK 15 billion, out of which 20 % are caused by cannabis; CZK 2,4
billion per year. Out of that the majority consists of crimes and administrative
infractions in connection with small quantities. It is estimated that the state could
save CZK 2 billion per year, provided that cannabis for one's own needs is legally
available in a regulated mode.
From the point of view of the intention of the lawmakers, public opinion must be
taken into account. Lawmakers as the elected representatives of the people
should primarily refect the democratic belief of the majority. The public opinion
polls conducted in 2016 show that over 55 % of the Czech citizens regard one-
time use of cannabis substances as a risk-free experience or one containing very
little risk and 22 % agree that the use of cannabis should be tolerated by society
and should not be prosecuted. Therefore, this legislation is to be only the frst
pilot project in harmony with the UN recommendation that should monitor further
development of cannabis use and provide data for the consideration of further
follow-up legislative steps.
In view of all the arguments above, the proposers believe that it is appropriate
and necessary to legalize growing small quantities of cannabis for the user's own
needs so that it is no longer classifed as unlawful conduct. By this regulation, the
lawmaker achieves a situation that from the legal point of view those people will
cease to carry out prohibited activities, to which, unfortunately on a mass scale,
they are now getting used as a result of the strict, but in reality not very
efective, legislation regarding administrative infractions in the area of addictive
substances.
The existing legal regulations contained in the Criminal Code remain unafected
by the proposed legislation. All crimes in relation to the abuse of addictive
substances remain in force without any changes, i.e. illegal production and other
types of handling of narcotic and psychotropic substances and poisons under
section 283, possession of narcotic and psychotropic substances and poisons
under section 284, illegal growing of plants containing narcotic and psychotropic
substances in greater than small quantity under section 285 and spreading
toxicomania under section 287 Act no. 40/2009 Sb., Criminal Code, as amended
("Criminal Code").
For the purposes of section 285 of the Criminal Code, there is no change in the
defnition of greater than small quantity, which is defned in Government Decree
no. 455/2009 Sb., in the extent of more than 5 cannabis plants and that remains
to be a crime under that provision. In reality, the only change is that it is
permitted (the prohibition is cancelled, it is legalized) to grow and dispose of a
small quantity of cannabis (i.e. up to 5 plants) for one's own needs, for certain
individuals (adult, without criminal record) and under precisely defned
conditions.
Present situation
Worldwide, at present there are tendencies consisting in reassessment of the
social and regulatory approach to cannabis. The policy of total criminalization of
cannabis transpires to be inefective, i.e. it does not lead to a decrease in the
numbers of administrative infractions or even criminal activities in relation to the
use of cannabis. The prohibition to possess cannabis and to use cannabis has
been lifted in nine federal sates of the United States of America (Alaska,
Colorado, California, Maine, Massachusetts, Nevada, Oregon, Vermont,
Washington). In those states growing, possessing and/or using cannabis for one's

own needs has been legalized and in some of them trading in this substance has
been legalized too.
Uruguay is an interesting precedent in this regard. Two years ago a law came
into efect permitting the growing of 5 cannabis plants at home based on
registration. In Uruguay, growing and selling cannabis in pharmacies is also
allowed under strict supervision. The state guarantees the quality and content of
substances contained in cannabis in order to minimize the risks that are posed
by using cannabis from the black market. At the moment, after two years since
the efect, the frst results of the legislation are obvious. More than 50 % of the
estimated illegal market are now under legal and tax control. The prices on the
black market dropped signifcantly under USD 1.5 for 1 gram, thus the
attractiveness of such contraband for organized crime groups has waned.
An important example is also Canada (a G7 member), where on 7 June 2018, Bill
no. C-45 was passed with its complex legislation for the broadest range of
possibilities of handling cannabis, including legislation governing the trade in
cannabis, its processing, import, export or sale, criminal liability and introducing
new prevention strategies. This law became an important model in preparing the
proposed legalisation of small quantity of cannabis for one's own needs in the
Czech Republic, some institutes were approached in a similar way, such as the
maximum quantity of cannabis that may be possessed in public (30 g) or the
concept of being able to grow, process and possess cannabis only within the
grower's dwelling. On the contrary, in an efort to prevent a turbulent
development, other institutes of the Canadian legislation were dismissed (such
as possession of cannabis by minors or permitting the sale of cannabis etc.).
The drug policy in the Czech Republic is managed by the Government Council for
Drug Policy Coordination, with the Antidrug Department of the Ofce of the
Government of the Czech Republic as its secretariat. The drug policy is
determined in the National Drug Policy Strategy for the period of 2010-2018. In
harmony with its long-term character, the Strategy defnes four general
objectives: (1) to reduce the level of experimental and occasional use of
addictive substances, particularly among young people; (2) to reduce the level of
problem and intensive use of addictive substances; (3) to reduce potential risks
related to the use of addictive substances to individuals and society, and (4) to
reduce the availability of addictive substances, particularly to young people. The
partial objectives, tools and activities for the achievement of the strategic
objectives are defned in more detail in regularly updated three-year action
plans. The situation in the Czech Republic, according to international reports and
studies, is comparable to that in other EU member states (also thanks to the
harmonization of rules within the unifed Single Market) and in many parameters
the problematic use of addictive substances in the Czech Republic has been
signifcantly below the mean values of the EU and the world.
The Czech Republic is one of the European Union countries where growing
cannabis in a small quantity (up to 5 plants) is not a crime, just an administrative
infraction. In Spain, Cyprus, Denmark, Belgium, the Netherlands or the United
Kingdom there is similar legislation. In those countries recreational use of
cannabis is quietly tolerated by society. Also, in the Czech Republic, society is
tolerant to cannabis users, thus it consciously does not accept the prohibition
imposed by the state or the fact that growing cannabis is classifed as an
administrative infraction and drying and processing cannabis is classifed as a
crime. The proposed legislation for a legal and controlled availability of cannabis
in the Czech Republic is an attempt at responding to the actual situation of the
users who grow cannabis, process or possess cannabis for their own needs and
in small quantities.
For other manners of handling cannabis, however, a stricter legal regulation was
chosen, e.g. compared to the Canadian legislation mentioned above, activities

such as sale, distribution, export and import of cannabis are completely
prohibited. Nevertheless the existing rules for cannabis used for therapeutic
purposes or in industry and research remain in force.
2. Impacts of the legalization of small quantity of cannabis for one's
own needs
A complete prohibition on growing cannabis, which is in place at present, is an
obstacle to the efective prevention and control of cannabis use among children
and the youth population. One of the key reasons for a complete loss of control
over the actual use of cannabis is the fact that it is disseminated illegally. An
undesirable attribute of the complete prohibition is the "forbidden fruit"
phenomenon, which makes cannabis even more attractive for minors.
International expert studies, such as "The Limited Relevance of Drug Policy:
Cannabis in Amsterdam and in San Francisco", brought the knowledge that there
is no evidence that can prove that the criminalization of cannabis decreases its
use across the population or that the legalization of cannabis increases the rate
of use. The studies also show that the long-term consumption of cannabis in
countries where the possession of cannabis is not illegal, does vary in a
signifcant way. No increase in the number of cannabis users is expected after
the adoption of the bill. Then again, the gateway drug theory, which regards
cannabis as a certain introduction to using other psychotropic substances, has
never been supported scientifcally either.
On the contrary, the practise shows that the lack of possibility to grow a few
cannabis plants on your own may be a reason to approach a dealer for the frst
time who usually deals other drugs as well. It may be anticipated that with the
partial legalization of growing, processing and possession of cannabis for one's
own needs the black market in cannabis will diminish and criminal activity
related to this issue will decrease. For that reason, it may be anticipated that the
costs in connection with hearing of administrative infractions and criminal
proceedings that are presently connected with punishments imposed on growers
of cannabis for their own needs will decrease (in 2015, 420 people were detained
due to possession and growing cannabis substances for their own needs,
according to information from the National Drug Enforcement Agency of the
Service of Criminal Police & Investigation of the Police of the Czech Republic, and
according to the Ministry of Justice of the Czech Republic, subsequently 60
people were convicted and sentenced to unconditional imprisonment under
section 284 Criminal Code).
This proposed bill will not have a negative impact on the Criminal Code (the
existing crimes in connection with cannabis remain unafected), on the contrary,
it will bring legal certainty by defning the term of "small quantity" of cannabis
and the rules for its growing, processing and possession for one's own needs.
Unfortunately, the established practice of the courts is still uncertain as far as
these rules are concerned, and the individual stages of handling cannabis are
assessed as diferent bodies of the crime with inadequate diferences in the
severity of punishment (in particular the problem is that part of the judicial
decisions tend to regard drying and possession of the plants grown on one's own
as stricter facts of the crime under section 283 Criminal Code, instead of milder
facts of the crime for the growing of cannabis under section 285 Criminal Code;
those uncertainties are not understood by the public and are criticized by
experts).
3. What the bill brings
The proposed legal regulation broadens the scope of application of the Act on
Addictive Substances, by its defnition of "small quantity" of cannabis and
legalizes growing, processing and possession of cannabis and cannabis products
for one's own needs. The proposed legislation introduces, for the purpose
specifed above, new defnitions of the terms "cannabis product", "cannabis

plant", "growing cannabis plants", "cannabis processing" and "possession of
cannabis or cannabis products".
- Cannabis and cannabis products for the purposes of growing, processing and
possession in small quantities for one's own needs specifed above, will be
subject to a special regime under section 24c Act on Addictive Substances. A
handling permit will not be required in those cases. The regime applicable to
research, scientifc and very limited therapeutic purposes defned in the handling
permit remains unchanged.
- Common conditions for growing cannabis plants, processing cannabis and
possessing cannabis and cannabis products will include no criminal record, legal
age and permanent residence in the Czech Republic. Growing cannabis plants
will be permitted if the conditions above are met and provided that the grower
grows the maximum of 5 cannabis plants. Growing cannabis will be permitted
only in certain predefned places and in a manner that prevents access to third
parties.
- Growing cannabis will be permitted in a dwelling (similar to Canada) or on land,
or in a building situated on such land, that is not publicly accessible and the use
of which by the grower is based on a private title.
Processing of the cannabis plants will be also permitted in a dwelling or on land,
or in a building situated on such land, that is not publicly accessible and the use
of which by the processor is based on a private title. Possession of cannabis or
cannabis products will be regulated in such a way that a quantity that the grower
is actually able to produce will be permitted (1250 g). Cannabis or cannabis
products may only be possessed within a dwelling to which a private title of use
of the possessor applies. Outside such a dwelling, individuals will be able to
possess a signifcantly smaller quantity of cannabis or cannabis products.
4. Assessment of the harmony of the proposed legislation with
international treaties
The proposed bill primarily concerns Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs of
1961, as amended. The Czechoslovak Socialist Republic expressed its agreement
with the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs on 31 July 1961 and it was signed
by the President on 23 November 1963 with reservations expressed upon the
execution. The Convention came into force on 13 December 1964 and on that
day it also came into force in the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic ("the
Convention"). On 25 March 1972 a Protocol amending the 1961 Single
Convention on Narcotic Drugs was agreed in Geneva and adopted by
Communication of the Federal Ministry of Foreign Afairs no. 458/1991 Sb.,
("Protocol"). Upon the handover of the instrument of ratifcation, the
Czechoslovak Socialist Republic negotiated an exception from the application of
selected provisions of the Convention (article 12, articles 2 and 3, article 13,
clause 2, article 14, clauses 1 and 2, and article 31, clause 1(b) of the
Convention), therefore they do not apply to the Czech Republic.
- The Convention does not prohibit the use of cannabis for one's own needs (or
rather does not mention this issue). The Convention does not prohibit legal
growing of cannabis but sets down the rules under which cannabis growing is
permitted - usually by introducing additional control mechanisms exercised by a
state agency (e.g. by the State Institute for Drug Control that exercises this
control under the Convention in regard to cannabis for therapeutic purposes).
The Convention does not apply to growing cannabis plants exclusively for
industrial purposes (for fbre and seeds) or for horticultural purposes. As far as
growing cannabis plants for personal needs is concerned, it is in fact growing for
horticultural purposes. Only by processing cannabis plants or cannabis products
constitute the elements of processing and possession of cannabis. Concerning
the processing of cannabis for your own needs in a small quantity, the

Convention does not expressly cover such a situation. It covers activities of a
much larger scale called manufacture (in the context of narcotic substances).
The Convention does not expressly defne any limits for possessing cannabis for
one's own needs, even though in some countries it is routine practice. It leaves
to the individual contracting states to defne the punishments and prevention
regarding cannabis.
- In accordance with the Convention, the Czech Republic meets the obligations
arising from the Convention as well as the general obligations and after the
adoption of the Bill, it will continue to restrict growing, processing and possession
of cannabis to a minimum extent, i.e. the small quantity, and only for the defned
purpose for one's own needs. The individual provisions of the Convention are
very general and are further implemented in accordance with the national will of
the individual contracting states. In this respect, the Czech Republic does not
change the existing legislation regarding addictive substances (in particular
concerning export, import, distribution and trade), it only adds a special regime
for cannabis grown, processed and possessed in small quantities for one's own
needs. Even though the Bill may appear to be in confict with the Convention in
partial activities, in the general context of the national drug policy of the Czech
Republic, it is a minor change to the existing legal regulation that follows the
development of the market, endeavouring to prevent further proliferation of
illegal trade in other addictive substances in the Czech Republic and the
proliferation of a cannabis black market.
II. Special section
Re article 1:
A new regulation is inserted in the Act on Addictive Substances to regulate
growing, processing and possession of cannabis and cannabis products in small
quantities for one's own needs. Such activities or procedures under predefned
conditions will be legalized. Up to now, those activities have constituted an
administrative infraction under the existing wording of the Act on Addictive
Substances [moreover, the processing collided with a somewhat absurd
construction of Act no. 40/2009 Sb., Criminal Code, as amended ("the Criminal
Code") that perceived drying of cannabis plants not as a natural continuation of
cannabis growing constituting milder elements of illegal growing of plants
containing a narcotic or psychotropic substance under section 285 Criminal Code,
but signifcantly stricter elements of illicit manufacture and other handling of
narcotic and psychotropic substances and poisons under section 283 Criminal
Code].
The proposed legal regulation determines the quantitative limits for growing and
possession of cannabis and cannabis products (including a diferentiation
between limits for the possession of cannabis or cannabis products in a dwelling
or outside a dwelling), thus it provides legal certainty as to the defnition of
"small quantity".
- The existing legal regulation of small quantity (or a greater than small quantity)
is, as far as the cannabis growing is concerned, governed by the Government
Decree no. 455/2009 Sb., which determines for the purposes of section 285 of
the Criminal Code what plants and fungi are regarded as plants and fungi
containing narcotic or psychotropic substances and what is a greater than small
quantity under the Criminal Code, as amended ("Government Decree no.
455/2009 Sb."). Presently growing 5 plants is not a crime under section 285
Criminal Code, but an administrative infraction under section 39  (2)(b) Act no.
167/1998 Sb., on Addictive Substances.
The defnition of small quantity (or greater than small quantity) for possessing
cannabis was determined for the purposes of sections 283, 284 and 286 Criminal

Code and in section 2 of Government Decree no. 467/2009 Sb., in which, for the
purposes of the Criminal, it is defned what substances are considered as poisons
and what is greater than small quantity in regard to narcotic substances,
psychotropic substances, preparations containing those substances and poisons,
in wording efective as of 22 August 2013. For cannabis, this limit was set in
Appendix 2 to more than 15 grams of dry matter (provided that it contains at
least 1.5 g of the efective psychotropic substance). Section 2 and Appendix no.
2, however, expired by the judgment of the Constitutional Court dated 23 July
2013, ref. no. Pl. ÚS 13/12, primarily because the defnition of small quantity (i.e.
the facts decisive in defning the facts of a crime) is insufcient in the statutory
instrument and must be defned exclusively by an Act.
In the present heterogeneous situation, small quantity is assessed in practice
mainly according to the statement of the Supreme Court Criminal Division (dated
13 March, 2014, ref. no. Tpjn 301/2013), where the Supreme Court determined a
certain orientation quantity. The proposed legal regulation of small quantity is
supported by reasons that a small quantity is not unambiguously defned by an
Act, which causes rather signifcant problems in practice and difering
approaches of the courts to this issue.
Re article 2:
The defnition of "cannabis product" is added. Cannabis products include dry
matter, because cannabis has to be processed (dried) after harvest. However, a
number of other products may also be a cannabis product (e.g. home-made
ointments) where cannabis is an ingredient. Cannabis as well as cannabis
products should be legal in small quantities for one's own needs. The defnition of
cannabis product then contains a non-exhaustive list of individual forms and
types of products that may be produced by cannabis processing.
A defnition of "cannabis plant" corresponds with the defnition in the Single
Convention on Narcotic Drugs ("Convention).
Growing cannabis plants is also partly based on the Convention and in a broader
way defnes the process that is considered as the growing stage.
The defnition of cannabis processing includes the term "production" contained in
the Convention, plus also drying, extracting and other processing of the cannabis
plant as well as extracting any cannabis products, According to the Convention,
production is strictly separable from manufacture, which, in view of the wording
of section 283 (Illegal production and other handling of narcotic and psychotropic
substances and poisons) et seq. of the Criminal Code, must be taken into
account in the Act on Addictive Substances. Professional literature states that:
"In practice the most frequent form of using cannabis to cause psychoactive
efects is its consumption (usually by smoking or inhaling, but also perorally) in
dried form of marijuana. This requires the process of harvesting, drying and
possibly crushing. The resulting product of this process - marijuana - however, is
not another narcotic psychotropic substance diferent form the original narcotic
substance of cannabis, it is still the same narcotic substance of cannabis, only in
another form, suitable for use (fresh vs. dried plant material). Besides, it is
appropriate to mention that the Single Convention defnes "production" (which is
not "manufacture") as, among others, the separation of cannabis and cannabis
resin from the plants from which they are obtained (the harvest of cannabis
plants to obtain cannabis falls into this defnition). For the reasons described
above, in my opinion, the process by which the cannabis plant is harvested, dried
and prepared to be used as marijuana, may not be considered as manufacture of
a narcotic psychotropic substance under section 283 Criminal Code." (ZEMAN,
Petr. „Výroba" konopí z konopí? Trestněprávní revue. 2015, No. 9, pp. 211-215).
At the same time, the defnition of possession of cannabis or cannabis products
is introduced. It is the stage that follows after growing and processing cannabis.

It is possession or another disposal of cannabis or cannabis products exclusively
for one's own needs. For the avoidance of doubt, it is expressly stated that the
possession of cannabis or cannabis products does not include handling addictive
substances under section 3 Act on Addictive Substances that applies to selected
activities in handling addictive substances as defned by the Act, i.e. not just
cannabis. At the same time, it is necessary to add that with the exception of
legal growing, processing and possession of cannabis in small quantities for
one's own needs, any other activities in connection with such cannabis, cannabis
plants or cannabis products are prohibited. Using cannabis for one's own needs is
not punishable.
Re article 3:
It arises from the nature of the matter that the legal regulations concerning
growing, processing and possession of cannabis and cannabis products for one's
own needs should be outside the scope of handling addictive substances (i.e. the
handling permit should not be required), as it entails many duties that would not
be ftting in this case. Moreover, from the structure of the legal regulations
concerning handling addictive substances, it arises that it is intended mainly for
legal entities and individuals carrying out business activities (the succession of
the elements of administrative infractions).
Even though it is a substance specifed Appendix no. 3 of Government Decree no.
463/2013 Sb., on the Lists of Addictive Substances, as amended, cannabis and
cannabis products for the purposes of growing, processing and possession in
small quantities for one's own needs should be regulated in a special way. The
general conditions for handling addictive substances and preparations would not
apply to them.
Re articles 4 and 5:
In view of the legal regulations contained in section 24c Act on Addictive
Substances, an amendment to prohibitions specifed in sections 15(e) and 24 (1)
(a) of the same Act is proposed.
The amendment to section 15 (e) Act on Addictive Substances refects the need
that the Act seeks to fulfl, to obtain cannabis products only in a legal manner.

Re article 6:
The proposed legal regulation inserts section 24c (c) which determines the
general prerequisites for the possibility of growing, processing and possession of
cannabis and cannabis products in small quantities for one's own needs. The
activities specifed therein may be carried out without a handling permit. The
basic conditions include:
•
no criminal record
•
majority
•
permanent residence in the Czech Republic.
The condition of permanent residence in the Czech Republic does not
discriminate against citizens of the European Union or third countries if their
permanent residence is in the Czech Republic. On the contrary, this condition
prevents possible "tourism" for the purpose of growing, processing and
possession of cannabis and cannabis products.

A person without a criminal record for the purpose of the Act is a person who has
not been convicted for an intentional ofence or is treated as if they have never
been convicted (under section 105 Act no. 40/2009 Sb., Criminal Code, as
amended).
For growing cannabis legally in compliance with the Act, it is necessary that the
individual does so in a dwelling or on land, or in a building situated on such land,
that is not publicly accessible and the use of which by the grower is based on a
private title (e.g. lease, ownership etc.). The private title to the use is essential so
that the growers may not grow cannabis in places, to which they have no
relationship, or in public areas. It is also necessary to grow cannabis in such
conditions that prevent access to the cannabis plants by third parties. If cannabis
is grown on land outside the dwelling, it is necessary that the land is not publicly
accessible.
The proposed legislation determines the highest legal quantity of cannabis plants
that may be grown by an individual (for more details see the explanation
concerning article 1). This quantity is based on the current legislation concerning
greater than small quantity defned in Appendix to Government Decree no.
455/2009 Sb. for the purposes of section 285 Criminal Code, as 5 cannabis
plants.
The proposed legal regulation also determines the highest legal quantity of dry
cannabis matter or cannabis products obtained from such quantity that may be
possessed for one's own needs. This quantity in reality represents "small
quantity" also for the purposes of the Criminal Code and the legal quantity that
may be possessed in a dwelling (to which the possessor holds a private title) is
strictly distinguished as well as the quantity that may be possessed outside a
dwelling.
Dwelling is defned in section 133 Act no. 40/2009 Sb., Criminal Code, as
amended, as a house, apartment or other premises used for housing and the
facilities belonging to it. It will be legally possible to possess the maximum of
1250 g of cannabis dry matter per person or cannabis products obtained from
such a quantity in a dwelling (to which the possessor holds a private title). This
value refects the quantity that may be obtained from fve cannabis plants that
may be legally grown in compliance with the law.
Outside a dwelling, it will be legally possible to possess the maximum of 30 g of
cannabis dry matter or cannabis products obtained from such a quantity.
Possessing the maximum of 1250 g of cannabis dry matter or cannabis products
obtained from such a quantity outside a dwelling is undesirable. The quantity is
based on the legal regulation introduced in Canada (the same quantity).
Exceeding this legal limit accomplishes the elements of the crime of possessing
narcotic and psychotropic substances or poisons under section 284 Criminal
Code.
Re article 7:
Newly, making available cannabis or cannabis products in breach of section 24c
(2) Act on Addictive Substances to third parties is newly considered as an
administrative infraction.
Re article II: Efect
The date of efect of the Act is proposed as the 15th day after the promulgation.
